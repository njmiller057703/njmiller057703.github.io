Project 3: Object Detection and Human-Object Interaction Analysis

By: Nicholas Miller

 

The objective of the project was to perform and implement various object detection models to deepen the understanding and capabilities of these object detection models. This project utilized a Single Shot Multi-Box Detector (SSD), Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS), and various open-source or closed-source Vision Language Models (VLM) to perform certain functions including object detection and Human-Object Interactions (HOI). The programs were run on Google Colaboratory to gain access of and utilize the GPU that is available to students to train the models.

 

The first portion of the project was implementing a lightweight object detection model. The purpose of this was to understand the capabilities of SSD’s or YOLO and many others. The banana detection dataset was used for this project to be the focus class of the detector. Figure 1 and Figure 2 possess a collection of various images containing a banana. The object detector struggles to predict the location of the banana in three out of the four images that are there.

 

Figure 1: Series of Images for the SSD

Figure 2: Second Series of Images for the SSD

 

The inaccurate detection of the bananas is due to a variety of cases regarding orientation, context of the image, color matching or differences, and many more. In the image on the far right, the banana is located at the top of one of the smokestacks where the object detector has difficulties predicting it because of the shape of the banana and the context of the image. As the images on the left, image one and image two, the theme was birds, and the banana was not detected in either case. The unsuccessful banana locating was due to the orientation of the banana and overpowering main object of the image. As this project isn’t about image classification, the original SSD with NMS seems to share properties regarding the imposing nature of the main object in the image being the focus of the image. Image 3 within the series of images in Figure 1 was able to locate the banana properly surrounding the banana with multiple bounding boxes. As seen in image 3, the confidences score obtained by the bounding boxes surrounding the banana were around 0.52. The confidence score of the bounding boxes being around 0.52 means that the SSD isn’t very confident that the bounding boxes contain the banana leading to uncertainty. This could also be a reason as to why the banana wasn’t located in the other images, the uncertainty of the content within the bounding boxes being too low will cause the bounding boxes not to show. Figure 3 contains the training loss plots for the epochs and the batches. A batch is a certain number of images, and the epoch refers to the number of times the model trains a batch of images.

 

Figure 3: Loss Function Graphs

 

Both plots for the training loss display a tendency to have a massive amount of loss at the beginning of the training where the curve flattens out towards the end of the training showing that the training loss lessens the more training the model undergoes. The difficulties that come with training is the immense amount of data necessary and the speed at which the model is able to train using this amount of data.

 

Part two utilizes the implementation of Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS). The NMS is designed to suppress overlapping bounding boxes and keep the boxes with the highest confidence scores. Figure 2 possesses images used to test the image detector with NMS. The confidence score of the object detector was 0.844. Figure 4 also contains a couple images displaying bananas to experiment with the NMS object detector with a confidence score of 0.789.

 

Figure 2: NMS Object Detector Image 1

 

Figure 3: NMS Object Detector Image 1

 

The confidence scores of the object detector with NMS for Figures 2 and 3 were 1.6 times higher than the object detector without NMS and 1.52 times higher than the object detector without NMS, respectively. The object detector with NMS possessed higher confidence scores because of the suppression of overlapping bounding boxes allowing for the object detector to have more accurate bounding boxes. The image in Figure 4 was an image acquired off the internet with the goal of demonstrating failure cases for the object detection model with NMS. The image contains two bananas cut and oriented in a certain way to appear as ducks or dolphins with grapes in their mouths. The fifth figure contains the results from the object detector with NMS which displayed that the model was unable to detect the bananas.

 

Figure 4: Internet Image for SSD

 

Figure 5: Failure Case for SSD

 

The object detection model was unable to determine the location of the bananas because of the uncertainty of the objects in the image. The model didn’t understand that the banana was cut at the stem as well as the eyes that were drawn on the bananas leading to a failure case of the understanding of the context of the image and the alterations conducted on the banana. When comparing the model to the implementation from PyTorch when utilizing NMS, the PyTorch method was simpler than the implementation used in this project. The NMS implementation used in this project was sufficient for determining the boxes, calculating the loss function, using the IoU threshold to suppress overlapping boxes, and then training the model. There are a few limitations to the object detector using NMS which seem to be understanding the context of the image to determine if an object is present, orientation causing the model to detect another object, color contrasting which hides objects, background versus foreground issues of depth perception, and time cost from training, box and confidence calculations, and efficiency.

 

The final part of the project was to utilize open-source or closed-source Vision Language Models to perform zero-shot HOI analysis on various images. The purpose of this activity was to observe the capabilities of the models and attempt to challenge the model to discover failure cases and other shortcomings. The Vision Language Model used to conduct this experiment was Gemini from Google. The images used to input into Gemini were sourced from a subset of HICO-DET called “Datasets at Hugging Face”. The sixth figure contains the first image used to experiment with Gemini.

 

Figure 6: Input Image One for Gemini

 

The prompt provided to Gemini to initiate the conversion was “Can you describe the Human-Object Interactions from the following image?”. Gemini proceeded to provide the major objects in the image, observed interactions, and implied interactions. For the major objects, Gemini provided the NASA spacecraft and the Boeing 747 aircraft. Gemini mentioned the interaction between the ground crew and the mobile airstairs used to board the aircraft. As for the implied interactions, Gemini refers to the task of attaching and securing the spacecraft to the aircraft. Figure 7 and Figure 8 show the output from Gemini regarding the Human-Object Interactions of the image from Figure 6.

 

Figure 7: Gemini Output for Input Image One Part One

 

Figure 8: Gemini Output for Input Image One Part Two

 

Figure 9 possess a second input image used to challenge Gemini. The image contains a man sitting on a couch next to a statue having a conversation in a park. The goal of this image was to deceive Gemini with one of the individuals being a statue acting as a potential therapist for the gentlemen.

 

 

Figure 9: Gemini Input Image Two

 

The prompt for this image was “Can you describe the Human-Object Interactions from the following image?”. The attempt to fool Gemini was unsuccessful, being able to identify the statue immediately. Gemini provided the same structured output as the previous output with the observed interactions and the implied interactions but excluding the major objects in the image. It was very difficult to deceive Gemini in the attempt to exploit the difficulties of Gemini. I was unable to trick Gemini because of the mass amount of training Gemini has undertaken with the use of the information gathered by Google. There may be shortcomings for some of these Vision Language Models like Gemini, ChatGPT, and others. It was very interesting to explore the capabilities of Gemini to observe its abilities in the advancement of Human-Object Interactions and object detection.
